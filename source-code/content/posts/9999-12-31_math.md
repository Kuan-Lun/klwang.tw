## 題目

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$, and let $W$ be a subspace of $V$. Show that there exists a linear transformation $T: V \mapsto V$ whose null space and range are both $W$ if and only if $\operatorname{dim} V = 2 \operatorname{dim} W$.

## 答題

Denote the null space of a linear function $f$ as $\mathrm{N}(f)$, and denote the range of a linear function $f$ as $\mathrm{R}(f)$. Denote the nullity of a linear function $f$ as $\operatorname{nullity}(f)$, and denote the rank of a linear function $f$ as $\operatorname{rank}(f)$. Write $n = \dim V$ and $k = \dim W$.

Suppose first that a linear transformation $T: V \to V$ exists with $\mathrm{N}(T) = \mathrm{R}(T) = W$. After the substitution of $V$ for $W$ in the dimension theorem (Friedberg et al. (2002), Theorem 2.3), we obtain

$$
\operatorname{nullity}(T) + \operatorname{rank}(T) = \dim V.
$$

Since $\mathrm{N}(T) = W = \mathrm{R}(T)$, both $\operatorname{nullity}(T)$ and $\operatorname{rank}(T)$ equal $\dim W = k$, so $k + k = n$, i.e. $n = 2k$. This proves necessity.

For the converse, suppose $\dim V = 2k$. Fix a basis $\{v_1, \ldots, v_k\}$ of $W$, which, viewed as a subset of $V$, is linearly independent. After the substitution of $\{v_1, \ldots, v_k\}$ for the linearly independent subset in the corollary to Theorem 1.11 (Friedberg et al. (2002)), we may extend $\{v_1, \ldots, v_k\}$ to a basis

$$
\beta = \{v_1, \ldots, v_k, v_{k+1}, \ldots, v_{2k}\}
$$

of $V$; exactly $k$ further vectors are needed, since $\dim V = 2k$.

After the substitution of $\beta$ for the basis, and of

$$
w_i = \begin{cases} 0 & 1 \le i \le k \\ v_{i-k} & k+1 \le i \le 2k \end{cases}
$$

for the prescribed target vectors, in Theorem 2.6 (Friedberg et al. (2002)), we obtain a unique linear transformation $T: V \to V$ satisfying

$$
T(v_i) = 0 \quad (1 \le i \le k), \qquad
T(v_{k+i}) = v_i \quad (1 \le i \le k),
$$

that is, $T$ sends the first half of $\beta$ to zero and the second half onto the basis vectors of $W$.

We now check that this $T$ has the required null space and range. After the substitution of $T$ and $\beta$ into Theorem 2.2 (Friedberg et al. (2002)), we obtain

$$
\mathrm{R}(T) = \operatorname{span}(T(\beta))
= \operatorname{span}(\{0,\ldots,0,v_1,\ldots,v_k\})
= \operatorname{span}(\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}) = W.
$$

For the null space, note first that $T(v_i) = 0$ for $1 \le i \le k$, so $v_1, \ldots, v_k \in \mathrm{N}(T)$ and hence $W \subseteq \mathrm{N}(T)$. After the substitution of $T$ for $f$ in the dimension theorem (Friedberg et al. (2002), Theorem 2.3) a second time, together with $\operatorname{rank}(T) = \dim W = k$ just established, we obtain

$$
\operatorname{nullity}(T) = \dim V - \operatorname{rank}(T) = 2k - k = k = \dim W.
$$

So $W$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $\mathrm{N}(T)$ with $\dim W = \dim \mathrm{N}(T)$. After the substitution of $\mathrm{N}(T)$ for $V$ in Theorem 1.11 (Friedberg et al. (2002)), we obtain

$$
W = \mathrm{N}(T).
$$

Combining the two computations, $\mathrm{N}(T) = \mathrm{R}(T) = W$, exactly as required. Together with the necessity argument above, this shows that a linear transformation $T: V \to V$ with null space and range both equal to $W$ exists if and only if $\dim V = 2\dim W$. $\blacksquare$

One boundary case is worth a remark: when $k = 0$, i.e. $W = \{0\}$, the condition $\dim V = 2k$ forces $V = \{0\}$ as well, and the zero map trivially satisfies $\mathrm{N}(T) = \mathrm{R}(T) = \{0\} = W$. The construction above degenerates gracefully to this case, since the basis extension step simply starts from the empty set.

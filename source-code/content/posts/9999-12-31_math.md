## 題目

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$, and let $W$ be a subspace of $V$. Show that there exists a linear transformation $T: V \mapsto V$ whose null space and range are both $W$ if and only if $\dim V = 2 \dim W$.

## 答題

Denote the null space of a linear function $f$ by $\mathrm{N}(f)$, the range of a linear function $f$ by $\mathrm{R}(f)$, the nullity of $f$ by $\operatorname{nullity}(f)$, and the rank of a linear function $f$ as $\operatorname{rank}(f)$.

Suppose first that there exists a linear transformation $T: V \mapsto V$ whose null space and range are both $W$. Then
$$
\operatorname{nullity}(T)=\dim W
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{rank}(T)=\dim W.
$$
By the dimension theorem (Friedberg et al. (2002), Theorem 2.3),
$$
\dim V
=\operatorname{nullity}(T)+\operatorname{rank}(T)
=2\dim W.
$$
This proves the necessity.

Conversely, suppose that $\dim V=2\dim W$.
Choose a subspace $U$ of $V$ such that
$$
V=W\oplus U.
$$
Then
$$
\dim U=\dim V-\dim W=\dim W.
$$
Hence, there exists an isomorphism \(S:U\to W\).

Every \(v\in V\) can be written uniquely as \(v=w+u\), where \(w\in W\) and \(u\in U\). Define \(T:V\to V\) by
$$
T(w+u)=S(u).
$$
This map is well-defined and linear. Since \(S\) maps \(U\) onto \(W\),
$$
\mathrm{R}(T)=S(U)=W.
$$
Moreover, because \(S\) is injective,
$$
T(w+u)=0
\iff S(u)=0
\iff u=0.
$$
Therefore,
$$
\mathrm{N}(T)=W.
$$
Thus, there exists a linear transformation \(T:V\to V\) whose null space and range are both \(W\). This proves the sufficiency and completes the proof.

## Reference

Friedberg, S. H., Insel, A. J., and Spence, L. E. (2002). Linear Algebra. *Pearson*, Upper Saddle River, 4th edition.

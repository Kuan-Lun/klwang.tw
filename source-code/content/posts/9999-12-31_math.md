## 題目

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $F$, and let $W$ be a subspace of $V$. Show that there exists a linear transformation $T: V \to V$ whose null space and range are both $W$ if and only if $\dim V = 2 \dim W$.

## 答題

Denote the nullity of $f$ by $\operatorname{nullity}(f)$, and the rank of a linear function $f$ as $\operatorname{rank}(f)$.

Suppose first that there exists a linear transformation $T: V \to V$ whose null space and range are both $W$. Then
$$
\operatorname{nullity}(T)=\dim W
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{rank}(T)=\dim W.
$$
By the dimension theorem (Friedberg et al. (2002), Theorem 2.3),
$$
\dim V
=\operatorname{nullity}(T)+\operatorname{rank}(T)
=2\dim W.
$$
This proves the necessity.

Denote the null space of a linear function $f$ by $\mathrm{N}(f)$, the range of $f$ by $\mathrm{R}(f)$.

Conversely, suppose that $\dim V=2\dim W$.
Extend a basis of $W$ to a basis of $V$, and let $U$ be the subspace spanned by the additional basis vectors.
Then
$$
\dim U=\dim V-\dim W=\dim W.
$$
After the substitution of $2$ for $k$, $W$ for $W_1$, and $U$ for $W_2$ in Friedberg et al.'s (2002) Theorem 5.10, every $v \in V$ can be written uniquely as $v=w+u$, where $w\in W$ and $u\in U$.
After the substitution of $U$ for $V$ in Friedberg et al.'s (2002) Theorem 2.19, $U$ is isomorphism to $W$.

Define $T:V\to V$ by
$$
T(w+u)=S(u).
$$
The uniqueness of the decomposition $v=w+u$ ensures that $T$ is well-defined, and the linearity of $S$ implies that $T$ is linear. Since $S(U)=W$,
$$
\mathrm{R}(T)=W.
$$
Moreover,
$$
T(w+u)=0
\iff S(u)=0
\iff u=0,
$$
where the last equivalence follows from the injectivity of $S$. Thus, $T(v)=0$ exactly when $v\in W$, so
$$
\mathrm{N}(T)=W.
$$
Therefore, there exists a linear transformation $T:V\to V$ whose null space and range are both $W$. This proves the sufficiency and completes the proof.

## Reference

Friedberg, S. H., Insel, A. J., and Spence, L. E. (2002). Linear Algebra. *Pearson*, Upper Saddle River, 4th edition.
